03Access, keys & safety

Alarm

Keysafes – two are required

Every home needs two keysafes before the first guest. We don't keep spare keys at the office, so if a guest locks themselves out or a cleaner needs the supplies cupboard, the keys have to be on site.

1 · Guest keysafe – holding one set of guest-only keys

It must be in a very obvious spot right by the front door, at eye level if possible. Guests arriving late will not go looking behind gates, around the side or in the garage, and a keysafe they can't find means calls to us and a bad start to their stay.

2 · Spare keysafe – holding a full spare set of keys plus the key to the locked supplies cupboard, for our cleaners and emergencies

If we install, we'll put the location and code in your welcome email once it's in. Keysafe costs are added to your first owner statement.

Keys: please provide two full sets of keys and remotes to every lock, door, window, garage and shed before the first guest arrives. If any are missing when we set up, we may need to have them cut and the cost is charged to you.

04Pets

Pet-friendly homes need somewhere a dog can be left securely outside. Please confirm:

We'll describe this in the listing so guests know what to expect. Without a secured area we usually advise against listing as pet friendly.

Our standard pet rules for guests
  • Maximum of two dogs, each up to 20 kg; larger dogs by request only.
  • Pets are kept off all furnishings and never left unsupervised inside.
  • Guests bring their own pet bedding, bowls and towels.
  • Pets left outside must be secure; they're the guest's responsibility, so secure fencing, or an area that can be secured, matters.
  • Before leaving, guests vacuum up pet hair inside and check the yard, bagging and binning any mess.
  • Guests check screens, doors and external points for pet damage and report or make good.
  • A $50 pet fee applies per booking with a pet (Airbnb).

These rules go in the guest guide and the listing for every pet-friendly home.

05Cleaning, linen & supplies

$350per clean · the standard package for every home

Charged to you after each guest departs; guests pay a flat $100 cleaning fee towards it, which comes back to you as booking income.

What a clean includes, and what it doesn't

Every clean: vacuum and mop throughout; bathrooms, showers and toilets; kitchen and all surfaces; a cosmetic sweep of courtyards, balconies and the barbecue area; bins; a photo of every room and a property check before the next arrival. Over time we also get to under-furniture, skirting boards, cupboard tidying and the front entrance.

Not part of a regular clean (deep-clean items, once or twice a year, by you on a visit or by us at an extra cost): windows inside and out, external cobwebs and external cleaning, pressure-washing decks, sugar-soaping walls, steam-cleaning grout, carpets and upholstery, heavy oven and barbecue cleaning, gutters, moving fridges, couches and beds to clean behind, exhaust fans and air-conditioner filters, and washing or dry-cleaning quilts.

Owner stays: you may clean after your own stay and the fee is waived; we check anyway while re-making beds and charge only for any extra time needed. Weekend or public-holiday cleans we can't move attract loadings (see the fee schedule).

Supplies we stock, included in the clean

We manage and restock the basics every holiday home should have, included in the clean: kitchen bin liners (4), bathroom bin liners (1 per bathroom), Chux (2), dishwasher tablets (8), dish cloth and scourer (1), toilet paper (2 per toilet), dishwashing detergent (1), body wash (1 per bathroom), spray and wipe (1), hand wash (1 per bathroom, 1 in the kitchen), tea bags (8), coffee sachets (8), sugar sachets (20), laundry detergent (1), salt, pepper and olive oil (1 each), paper towel (1), fly spray (1), long-life milk (2), shampoo and conditioner (1 each), cling wrap and foil (1 each), matches (1) and firelighters where there's a fireplace, and the arrival gift: a bottle of wine and a welcome note. We use Who Gives a Crap toilet paper, Biology spa-range bathroom products and eco-friendly cleaning products wherever possible. The package may change over time.

You supply: firewood and kindling, coffee pods or beans, boxed tissues, remote batteries, light globes and heat bulbs, gas bottles, fabric softener and stain remover, pegs, hairdryers, vacuum bags, mop heads and brooms, barbecue cleaning products, phone chargers, spices and sauces, dishwasher deep-clean tablets, oven and timber cleaners. We'll let you know when essentials like firewood run low.

Owner's stock cupboard: a lockable cupboard or space we can reach between bookings makes a real difference. Keep in it spare mattress and pillow protectors, a spare pillow or two, a spare quilt and blankets, spare globes for the lights in the house, batteries for the remotes, a spare set of glasses and wine glasses, and spare plates and bowls, so we can replace chips and breakages like for like. It isn't essential; the alternative is we order as needed, usually with a week's delivery lag.

Bins

Red and yellow bins are required. Our team takes bins in and out when we're there for a clean or check, and guests help; we don't make special trips. If guests leave on a day when collection is more than 48 hours away and the bins are full, we may need to charge for emptying them so the next guests have somewhere for their rubbish; the same can happen in summer when a bin simply can't keep up.

What a bin concierge does: a local service that comes every week on collection day to wheel your bins out to the kerb the night before and bring them back in afterwards, whether or not anyone is staying, for about $10–15 a week. It suits homes on main roads where bins can't be left out, or in high-wind spots where they blow over, and homes with sensitive neighbours. For most homes our staff and guests manage bins well enough without it.

07How we look after repairs and the home

Urgent and non-urgent repairs

Urgent (gas leak, burst pipe, major electrical fault, a safety risk): we try you and your emergency contact first; if we can't reach you we act to make the home safe, up to $3,300 (a burst hot-water service, for example).

Non-urgent: globes, minor handyman items, replacing broken glasses, stained protectors or a dead kettle. We attend to these between bookings within reason and tell you what we did; under $200 we act without checking first when a guest is arriving and trades are hard to line up.

Bottled gas, wood fires, gas appliances and smoke alarms

Bottled gas: we check levels at every clean and arrange refills through Apollo Bay Gas & Electrical unless you tell us otherwise by email.

Wood fire: a lockable wood store is ideal; we then supply enough for a 2–3 night stay and restock firewood and kindling at your cost (see fees). Wood is supplied to guests April to November as standard (about 3 m³ over a winter, depending on bookings); year round if the fire is the only heating. You provide an ash bucket, wheelbarrow, splitter and spare, and kindling stock.

Gas appliances such as heaters and stoves should be maintained by a licensed gas fitter; unserviced appliances can pose a carbon-monoxide risk, and Energy Safe Victoria recommends a service at least every two years; smoke alarms tested and batteries replaced yearly and units replaced every ten years. Both are the owner's responsibility (your insurance may depend on it); we're happy to arrange checks if you ask.

Our current standard policies

Cancellation: Airbnb's Moderate policy, a full refund up to five days before arrival; it has proven effective for forward bookings and may be adjusted with the market.

Length-of-stay discounts on every channel: 10% for 3 nights, 15% for 4–6, 20% for a week, 25% for a month. Nightly rates ease up to 5% inside the last two weeks if a date is still open; we don't stack early-bird or last-minute offers on top.

Launch: 20% off the first three Airbnb bookings to seed reviews. Winter: we may run offers in low demand to hold search ranking; tell us if you'd rather not. Cleaning fee: guests pay a flat $100 per stay, part of your booking income.

Airbnb's algorithms reward continuous bookings, value and guest experience more than any single price, so you may see pricing vary while the listing establishes or in quiet periods.

What to expect in the first year

Launching between April and November takes time. Homes with views, rural settings, spas, saunas, pools or play areas book consistently on weekends; homes without them lean on long weekends and school holidays, which is why presentation and photography matter so much.

Early on we price for value to win bookings and reviews, then lift rates as performance builds. Winter running costs can exceed income; the bulk of the year's profit sits in summer. Owners who stay in dialogue with us, keep presentation at guest standard and act on feedback see the best results.
