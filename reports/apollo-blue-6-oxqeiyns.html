What we recommend

Changes to pricing, your listing and the house, based on the market and on what guests are telling us. Nothing changes until you tell us. Reply to the email this came with.

  1. 1

    Pricing

    Let late November and early December weeknights go a little lower

    The next 30 days are 13% booked against 24% for comparable homes. Your minimum of $330 keeps quiet weeknights from easing much below the market median.

    Our suggestion: Lower the minimum nightly rate to about $296 (the market's lower quarter for late November) until mid-December, so midweek gaps can fill. Weekends will still price higher automatically.

    To go ahead, reply “yes to 1”.

  2. 2

    Pricing

    Mid to late January: hold until 1 December, then ease

    11–31 January is all still open and priced above the median ($677 against $547), including the Australia Day weekend at $990.

    Our suggestion: Hold current rates until 1 December. Then ease any nights still open towards $547 (the market median) for midweek January, keeping the Australia Day weekend between $703 and $990 until mid-January.

    To go ahead, reply “yes to 2”.

  3. 3

    Pricing

    Review your maximum for next summer's peak dates

    Your maximum nightly rate is $990. Over Christmas, New Year and early January, PriceLabs would have priced most nights well above it, up to $1,642 on 28 December, and those dates booked months ahead.

    Our suggestion: Consider a higher maximum for peak dates only (Christmas to mid-January and long weekends) for next season, so the busiest nights can earn what the market will pay. Other dates would be unaffected.

    To go ahead, reply “yes to 3”.
