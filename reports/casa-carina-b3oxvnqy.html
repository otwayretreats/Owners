You asked

“Can you review our pricing overall? We feel it has been set too high.”

We've looked at every open night for the next three months against comparable homes nearby. For November and early December your rates are actually below the market: 6–30 November averages $315 a night against a median of $340, and 1–9 December averages $347 against $359. Most weeknights are sitting on your $300 minimum, which is above the market's lower quarter of $281 for November, so that floor is the main thing holding rates up.

Summer is where you're priced above the middle of the market: 23–31 December is at your maximum of $900 (median $715), and January averages $751 (median $588). Those are still below the top quarter, and it's normal for summer to book later, but you're 16% booked over the next 90 days against 30% for comparable homes, so we don't want to leave it too late.

Our three suggestions below respond directly to this: a slightly lower minimum for quiet weeknights now (1), and set dates to ease Christmas–New Year (2) and January (3) if they're still open.

The season so far

We're in the quiet run between the spring long weekends and the summer holidays, when Apollo Bay books late and mostly on weekends. Across the next 60 days you're 23% booked against 29% for comparable homes, so the gap is mostly in the next few weeks rather than over summer.

The pricing tools (midweek pricing, booking-pace adjustment and last-minute easing) are already pulling rates down while the calendar is quiet; the minimum is what stops them going lower. That's why our first suggestion is about the minimum rather than the summer rates.
