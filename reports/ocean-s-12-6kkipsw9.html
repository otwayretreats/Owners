What we recommend

Changes to pricing, your listing and the house, based on the market and on what guests are telling us. Nothing changes until you tell us. Reply to the email this came with.

  1. 1

    Pricing

    November and early December: allow one-night midweek stays

    No nights are booked in the next 30 days, while comparable homes are 27% booked. Rates are already just below the market median ($305 against $321), so price isn't the main barrier; the two-night minimum on quiet weeknights is.

    Our suggestion: Allow one-night stays from Sunday to Thursday until mid-December, keeping two nights on weekends. Early stays bring the first reviews, which help the home win summer bookings.

    To go ahead, reply “yes to 1”.

  2. 2

    Pricing

    Summer: price Christmas to January between $529 and $990, easing as dates approach

    January averages $774, in the market's top quarter, and nothing is booked yet. A new home without reviews usually needs to sit a little closer to the market to win its first summer stays.

    Our suggestion: Hold current rates until 1 December, then ease any nights still open towards $687 (the market's top quarter), and towards $529 (the median) for nights still open inside two weeks.

    To go ahead, reply “yes to 2”.

The season so far

Summer bookings are coming in along the coast: comparable homes around Skenes Creek are about 31% booked for the next 60 to 90 days. Ocean's 12 has only just joined us, and the first few stays matter most, because they bring the reviews that let us price higher through the season.

We'll watch the calendar closely and let you know as soon as the first stays land.
