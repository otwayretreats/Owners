Minimum price, $350. PriceLabs never sets a nightly rate below this, even for a quiet night that hasn't booked close to the date. It protects the value of your home; set too high, quiet nights are more likely to stay empty. Booking site promotions are applied after this, so a discounted booking can pay out a little less per night.

Base price, $450. PriceLabs moves each night up or down from the base for the season, weekends and weekdays, local events and demand, and how quickly your calendar is filling. Guests rarely see the base price itself; raising or lowering it shifts every night.

Maximum price, $900. PriceLabs never sets a rate above this, even on the busiest nights such as New Year's Eve. If peak nights keep reaching the maximum and still book, there may be room to lift it.

Your base of $450 a night sits right on PriceLabs' own recommendation of $446. Every open night in the next week is at your $350 minimum, which is the floor doing its job in a quiet patch.

What this means for your minimum stay. Long Christmas stays are booked early: last season the 7-night-plus bookings were made a median 97 days ahead, while 3–4 night stays were booked 46 days ahead and 47% of them inside the last six weeks; 1–2 night stays were booked 19 days out. By this point last year 100 Christmas stays were on the books across our homes, and the season finished with 208: nine in ten of the bookings made after this point were 1–4 night stays. A 7-night minimum held into December protects the chance of one long booking, but it also turns away the shorter stays that now make up most of the demand. Our usual approach is a longer minimum early, stepping down to 3 or 4 nights about six weeks out, and 2 nights to fill gaps in the last fortnight; we set this in PriceLabs so it happens automatically.
